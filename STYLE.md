# House style

How Café Security Research writes. The aim is plain, exact, evidence-first prose that a busy reader trusts. This applies to every page, report and claim.

## Base references

- **Spelling and grammar:** British English (-ise, -our, "whilst" avoided, single quotes for speech). When in doubt, follow the **GOV.UK style guide** — it is written for plain English and a broad professional audience, and has an A-to-Z of words to avoid.
- **Tone:** short, concrete and sceptical of hype, in the spirit of **The Economist Style Guide**. State the fact, cite it, move on.

## Principles

1. **Say it plainly.** Prefer the short word to the long one, the concrete to the abstract. "Use", not "utilise" or "leverage". "Enough", not "sufficient".
2. **Evidence over adjectives.** A claim earns its place with a source or a file-and-line reference, not with words like "powerful", "sophisticated" or "robust".
3. **No hype, no doom.** The subject is serious enough without inflation. Where the news is bad, say so and then say what to do about it.
4. **Active voice, short sentences.** Name who does what. Break a long sentence in two.
5. **Define a term once, then use it consistently.** Don't vary "finding / result / discovery" for elegance; pick one.

## Words and phrases we don't use

These read as filler or as machine-written text. Cut them or replace with the plain word.

- **Never:** land (as in "how this lands"), delve, leverage (as a verb), harness (as a verb), unlock, realm, landscape (as a metaphor), tapestry, cutting-edge, game-changer, seamless, robust (as vague praise), utilise, foster, navigate (as a metaphor), underscore, showcase, pivotal, crucial (as filler), vibrant, ever-evolving, in today's fast-paced/ever-changing world.
- **Constructions to avoid:** "It's not X — it's Y" as a reveal; "Here's the truth nobody tells you"; opening with "In an era of…"; the rule-of-three flourish repeated for rhythm; a paragraph of three short punchy fragments for drama.
- **Swapping one tired word for another doesn't help.** "Utilise" is no better than "leverage". Reach for the plain word: "use".

## Light touches

- Dry humour is allowed, sparingly, and every instance must be removable without loss.
- Em dashes are fine in moderation; don't let them become a tic.
- Numbers: spell out one to nine, figures for 10 and above, except where a unit or comparison makes figures clearer.

## Checklist before publishing a page

- Read it aloud. Anything you wouldn't say to a colleague, rewrite.
- Every factual claim cites a source or a claim ID.
- No word from the "don't use" list survived.
- British English throughout.
- The bad news is paired with what to do about it.
